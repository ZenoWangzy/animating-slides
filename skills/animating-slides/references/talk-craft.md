# Talk craft: the storyline comes first

A beautiful deck can't rescue a story that doesn't hold. Settle the story before you draw a pixel.

## The shape

- **Throughline.** Write one sentence, under about 15 words, that the audience should carry out of the room. Every beat serves it. (Chris Anderson, *TED Talks*.)
- **The hook is a fact, not an ad.** Open on something surprising and concrete: a number, a before and after, a single object. Don't open on the product, because people tune out an ad.
- **Problem, learning, result.** People follow a story where someone meets a problem, figures something out and gets a result. A list of features doesn't hold them.
- **What is, then what could be.** Alternate the painful present with the possible future, and land on the future. (Nancy Duarte, *Resonate*.)
- **Concrete beats abstract.** "A customer waits three days for a refund" lands; "slow support processes" doesn't. (Chip and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick*.)
- **Reset attention about every 8 to 10 minutes** with a turn, a reveal, a short video or a joke that comes from the story.
- **Rule of three, then callback.** List three things, and let the end come back to the opening image. Ending on the picture you opened with lands better than ending on a summary list.
- **The end.** Close on one line and stop. A recap of every point sounds like a lecture.

## The audience

People listen for things that are interesting, things that make or save them money, and things that touch their own work. Cut any beat that serves none of these, however proud the speaker is of it.

## Beats

- One beat is one click and one idea. Write `Say` the way the speaker talks, not as prose.
- `Point` is what the picture must say on its own. If you can't write it in one line, the beat holds two ideas, so split it.
- `Source` covers every fact: the speaker's own words, a file, a dataset or a URL. Ask the speaker when the story needs a fact you don't have.
- Keep the jokes the speaker approves. A joke that falls flat on paper falls harder on stage.

## Live demos and video

- Show one main path only. Record it as a backup video and keep key frames as stills in the deck.
- Use a product video as evidence after the story has made people curious, never as the opening.
